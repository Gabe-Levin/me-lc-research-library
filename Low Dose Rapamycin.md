# Low Dose Rapamycin (Sirolimus)

mTOR inhibitor. Approved for organ transplant rejection / certain cancers; increasingly used off-label at low dose (~5 mg/week) for healthspan and immune modulation.

## Mechanisms relevant to [[ME-CFS]] / [[Long COVID]]

- ↓ inflammatory cytokines / immune modulation
- **Antiviral properties** — relevant to [[Viral Persistence and Reactivation]] (LC linked to spike persistence, [[EBV]] reactivation, T-cell exhaustion)
- ↑ ATP, induces autophagy/mitophagy → benefits [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]
- **Microglial mTOR target** → [[Neuroinflammation]]

## Per [[Dr Putrino]]

"When pathogens are reactivating... immunomodulators become important." Refers to rapamycin in context of LC patients with reactivated HHVs + parasites + Borrelia/Bartonella/Babesia.

## Studies

- **Hatfield et al. 2025 pre-print** — Low-dose rapamycin in ME/CFS via autophagy improvement — alleviated fatigue and PEM
- **Acute COVID-19 (2023)**: continuous rapamycin users had significant reduction in moderate/severe disease; zero LC cases despite 7.5y older average age
- **Mannick 2014**: 1x weekly Everolimus 5mg × 6 weeks → improved flu vaccine response + ↓ T-cell exhaustion markers in healthy older adults
- **Putrino LC trial in progress** — low-dose rapamycin RCT, completion mid-2026

## Risks / side effects

- Major lifespan trials: mouth sores (15% vs 5% control); no other significant increases
- **Skeletal muscle risk** — acute administration inhibits muscle protein synthesis. Concern for older patients. Pair with strength rehab.
