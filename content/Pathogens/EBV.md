# EBV — Epstein-Barr Virus (HHV-4)

Once infected, lifelong. Reactivates under stress / immune suppression. Causes mononucleosis acutely; reactivation implicated in [[ME-CFS]] and [[Long COVID]] (also MS, lupus, some cancers).

## Diagnostics

- **VCA IgM** — active/recent infection
- **VCA IgG** — historic immunity (positive lifelong)
- **EA-D IgG (early antigen, diffuse)** — *recent reactivation* marker (transient: 2–3 months after viral activity)
- **EBNA IgG** — appears late; rise can mark reactivation
- PCR for active replication
- Armin Labs Eli-Spot (T-cell function)

## Evidence in [[Long COVID]]

- **Gold et al. 2021**: 66.7% LC subjects vs 10% controls positive for EA-D IgG or VCA IgM
- **Hentschel 2023**: EBV more common in LC fatigue cohort vs convalescent controls — possible co-factor in subgroup
- **Chen 2021 (acute COVID)**: 55.2% of 67 acute patients had VCA IgM positive; associated with worse disease

## Evidence in [[ME-CFS]]

- **Montoya 2012 (n=30)**: high IgG titers for EBV + [[HHV-6]] treated 6mo with valganciclovir — many improved
- **Lerner studies (2002–2010)**: [[Valacyclovir]] 4g/day in EBV+ ME/CFS; improvements over 5–6 months; 10–25% needed lifelong therapy

## Acquired immunodeficiency framework (Ruiz-Pablos 2023)

Proposes EBV reactivation creates an acquired immunodeficiency:
1. Weak EBV HLA-II haplotype → loss of latency I control
2. Ectopic lymphoid structures form in tissues (including CNS) → inflammation, impaired T-cell function
3. Chronic antigen exposure → immune exhaustion → disease consolidation

Therapy stack proposed: antioxidants ([[Oxidative Stress]] reduction) + [[Antivirals]] + metformin.

## Treatments targeting EBV

- [[Valacyclovir]] (1g q6h — Lerner)
- [[Acyclovir]]
- [[Famciclovir]]
- [[Valganciclovir]] (also CMV)
- [[Truvada]] (tenofovir; very potent EBV polymerase inhibitor — Drosu 2020)
- IMC-2 ([[Valacyclovir]] + celecoxib) — Dogwood positive interim 2024
- High-dose IV vitamin C — observational evidence of titer reduction
- Supplements: L-lysine, astragalus, quercetin, Viracid, monolaurin (weak evidence)
- **[[HBOT]] caveat**: may be detrimental — promotes latent cell expansion
