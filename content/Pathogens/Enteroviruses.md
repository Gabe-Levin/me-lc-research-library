# Enteroviruses

Family including coxsackievirus A/B, echovirus, poliovirus, rhinovirus. Major hypothesis for [[ME-CFS]] root cause — championed by [[Dr Chia]] and reviewed by [[Dr Hanson]].

## Why suspected

- Historic ME/CFS cluster outbreaks coincided geographically/temporally with polio outbreaks (Hanson 2021/2023)
- Common: CDC estimates 10–15M US infections/year
- Many are asymptomatic → likely under-attributed as illness triggers
- Most acute viruses can't go latent; Chia proposes **non-cytolytic mutant enteroviruses** that persist at low levels (reduced positive:negative strand ratio in ME/CFS tissues)

## Chia's clinical findings

- 95% abdominal tenderness in clinic patients
- 82% positive on stomach biopsy vs 20% controls (Chia 2008)
- Brain autopsy: EV detected in deceased ME/CFS patient (Chia 2015)
- Tropism: CNS, heart, muscles — only virus that hits all 3
- Mitochondrial impact: CVB1 in PANC-1 cells downregulates oxidative phosphorylation, FA oxidation, Krebs cycle proteins

## Testing

- **ARUP** Coxsackievirus + Echovirus neutralizing antibody panels — Chia specifies ARUP; LabCorp insufficient
- Titres ≥1:160–1:320 suggest chronic infection
- Stomach biopsy (Chia's preferred)
- Insurance friction; Quest may refuse to forward to ARUP

## Treatments (per Chia)

### Chinese herbs (1st line)
- **[[Oxymatrine Equilibrant]]** — Equilibrant is Chia's proprietary formula; 30–56% response rate over many years
- **[[Dihydroquercetin]]** (taxifolin) — efficacy vs CVB4; some patients with POTS resolution within a month

### Rifampin augmentation
- 7-day course with Equilibrant; 67% develop flu-like reaction; 70% of responders had further improvement; 64% able to discontinue herbs

### Interferon (rarely used now)
- α + γ; 40–50% effective at 3 months; $5,000/mo; intolerable side effects; relapse on discontinuation

### IVIG
- ~30% response, helps myalgia/fatigue; expensive, insurance battle

### Ribavirin
- 7/10 improved in early Chia study; relapse 1–2 weeks after discontinuation

### [[Remdesivir]] (2025 study)
- 75% (15/20) ME/CFS with chronic EV infection achieved remission
- 52% (104/200) response with Paxlovid in ME/CFS EV cohort
- Phase results awaiting full peer review
