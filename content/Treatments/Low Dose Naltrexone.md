# Low Dose Naltrexone (LDN)

Off-label use of 1.5–4.5 mg naltrexone (vs the 50 mg addiction dose). Cheap, well-tolerated, broad immunomodulation. Most ME-literate clinicians consider it worth a 6-month trial.

## Proposed mechanisms

- TLR4 antagonism → microglial inhibition (anti-[[Neuroinflammation]])
- Transient opioid receptor blockade → endorphin rebound
- Mast cell modulation (helps [[MCAS]] overlap)
- TRPM3 ion channel modulation (Marshall-Smith framework for ME/CFS)

## Studies — [[ME-CFS]]

- **Polo 2019** retrospective (n=218 Finland): 74% self-reported positive response, 18% no effect
- **Bolton 2020** case series of 3: improved energy, sleep, mood, pain — dose-sensitive

## Studies — [[Long COVID]]

- **Isman 2024** (n=36 mod/severe LC): LDN + [[NAD Plus]] patches → SF-36 36→52; ~½ responders
- **Bonilla 2023** retrospective (n=108): LDN improved fatigue + pain; amitriptyline improved fatigue only
- **O'Kelly 2023** retrospective (n=59): fewer symptoms, improved fatigue/PEM/sleep/function
- **Olwagen 2022** (n=38): improvements across daily activities, energy, pain, sleep, concentration; pain-related largest

## Studies — [[Fibromyalgia]]

- **Younger 2010 pilot** (n=10): 30%+ symptom reduction over placebo
- **Younger 2013 RCT** (n=30): 30% pain ↓ vs 18% placebo
- Higher sedimentation rate predicted greater response (inflammatory subset)

## Studies — other

- **Younger 2018** crossover RCT in Gulf War Illness — positive

## Centrality finding (Eastep 2025 PNAS, n=3,925)

**LDN was the 2nd-highest-rated treatment overall** in patient-reported survey: 49.4% positive; fatigue 41.5%, PEM 33.2%, brain fog 42.3%. Conversely **GET was the most harmful (-72.2% NAS)**.

## Practical

- Build up 1.5 → 3 → 4.5 mg over weeks
- Can source from RTHM Direct or compounding pharmacy
- Mostly bedtime dosing (vivid dreams initially common)
