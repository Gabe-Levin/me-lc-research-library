# Autonomic Autoantibodies

Autoantibodies against autonomic nervous system receptors (ANSRs):
- **β1-adrenergic** (>15 U/mL)
- **β2-adrenergic** (>8 U/mL)
- **M3 muscarinic acetylcholine** (>6 U/mL)
- **M4 muscarinic acetylcholine** (>10.7 U/mL)

Plus anti-AT-II (angiotensin), anti-nociceptin in some studies.

## Why they matter

- May disrupt autonomic signaling → [[POTS]], orthostatic intolerance, vasoregulation problems
- Elevated levels correlate with symptom severity in [[Long COVID]] and [[ME-CFS]]
- Correlate with structural CNS alterations
- **Mechanistic basis for [[Immunoadsorption]] trials** — removing IgG removes the antibodies

## Studies

- **Bynke 2020 ME/CFS pilot (n=10)**: IA → 7/10 had improvement lasting up to 12 months in some patients
- **Sotzny 2024 post-COVID ME/CFS (n=20)**: IA in patients with elevated β2-AB → 70% responders by SF-36 PF, improvement peaks at months 2–3, declines after
- **IA-PACS-CFS RCT (Dec 2024 interim, n=12)**: 7/10 had ≥10pt SF-36 PF improvement at week 4
- Antibody levels rebound ~1 month after IA → repeat cycles needed

## Treatments targeting this mechanism

- [[Immunoadsorption]] (Germany-centric)
- [[IVIG SCIG]] — neutralizes/modulates
- [[Low Dose Rapamycin]] — mTOR inhibition, immune modulation
- Plasmapheresis (one case study showed near-complete resolution post-IA2)
