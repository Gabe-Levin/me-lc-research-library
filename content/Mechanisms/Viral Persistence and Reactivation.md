# Viral Persistence and Reactivation

A proposed root-cause cluster for [[ME-CFS]] / [[Long COVID]]:
- **Persistence** — virus (or fragments) remaining in tissue after acute infection
- **Reactivation** — latent virus re-emerging when immunity is suppressed

## Persistence in [[Long COVID]]

- **Swank et al. 2022, Nature Communications**: persistent SARS-CoV-2 spike protein months after acute infection
- **2022 paper**: SARS-CoV-2 directly interacts with mitochondria, causes structural damage that may persist after viral clearance → see [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]
- LC patient muscle biopsies: SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid detected but not statistically different from controls (2024 PEM study) — viral *persistence* may not directly cause PEM

## Reactivation patterns

In LC and ME, multiple herpesviruses can flare:
- **[[EBV]]** — most studied; ~⅔ of LC patients show EA-D IgG positivity (Gold 2021)
- **[[HHV-6]]** — proposed in ME by Montoya et al.
- **[[CMV]]** — less commonly framed but co-infections reported in LC

[[Dr Putrino]] frame: even apparently "simple" LC cases turn out to have multiple pathogens active (HHVs + vector-borne) by the time of presentation. Awaiting full pathobiology publication.

## Diagnostic challenges

- IgG titres remain elevated lifelong; rising titres or EA-D IgG more specific for reactivation
- PCR may miss intracellular / low-load reactivation
- Tissue-level detection often needed (e.g., [[Dr Chia]] stomach biopsy for [[Enteroviruses]])

## Treatment options

[[Antivirals]] — broad category · [[Valacyclovir]] · [[Acyclovir]] · [[Famciclovir]] · [[Valganciclovir]] (CMV) · [[Truvada]] (EBV via tenofovir) · [[Remdesivir]] · [[Paxlovid]] · [[Oxymatrine Equilibrant]] (enteroviruses) · [[Dihydroquercetin]] · [[IVIG SCIG]] (immune support) · [[Low Dose Rapamycin]] (mTOR + antiviral) · high-dose vitamin C (EBV)

## Caveat on [[HBOT]]

May be beneficial for non-latency viruses (SARS-CoV-2) but **possibly detrimental for latent herpesviruses** — promotes latent cell expansion via oxidative stress. Open question.
